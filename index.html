Existing Equipment
Capture all nameplate data from existing units. Take a photo of each nameplate — don't rely on handwriting alone.
📋 Where to find nameplate data
💡 Photograph every nameplate before recording. If the plate is worn or unreadable, note that — it affects warranty and refrigerant records.
Existing Packaged Unit
A packaged unit contains all components in one cabinet — compressor, coil, and air handler together. One nameplate covers everything.
Existing Outdoor Unit (Condenser / Heat Pump)
MCA and MOCP are on the condenser nameplate. MCA = minimum wire ampacity. MOCP = maximum breaker/fuse size. These determine if the existing electrical can support the new unit.
Existing Indoor Unit (Air Handler / Furnace)
90%+ furnace produces condensate — confirm drain routing before ordering.
⚠️ Replacing an 80% furnace with a 90%+ unit requires new PVC venting, a condensate drain, and a combustion air intake pipe. Add to job scope before pricing.

Electrical
Electrical mismatches cause the most install day delays. Capture everything here so the right disconnect, wire, and breaker are on the truck.
⚡ Electrical terms explained
💡 The breaker for the condenser must be between MCA and MOCP of the new unit. The wire gauge must handle MCA. Wrong disconnect = install day delay. This is the #3 cause of install day problems.
Electrical Panel
⚠️ Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels are known safety concerns. Flag these for the install manager immediately.
Condenser Disconnect
Capacity: #14=15A · #12=20A · #10=30A · #8=40A · #6=55A · #4=70A. Wire must handle the new unit's MCA.
How to identify it: Open the outdoor disconnect box (small weatherproof box on the wall near the condenser) — the gauge is printed on the cable jacket, e.g. 10 AWG or 10/2 WITH GROUND. If inaccessible, use breaker size as a guide: 30A breaker → likely #10  ·  20A → likely #12  ·  40A → likely #8. When in doubt, select “Unknown.”

Toggle on everything present in this home — estimates are pre-filled and editable.

ℹ️  What this calculation already includes (NEC 220.82 Optional Method)
The following are automatically calculated from the home's square footage — do not add these again:
  • General lighting & receptacles — all standard 15A/20A outlet and lighting circuits are covered by the 3 VA/sq ft general load (NEC 220.12). Individual receptacle or lighting circuits are not counted separately.
  • Small appliance circuits ×2 — the two required kitchen/dining branch circuits (NEC 220.52(A)) are pre-included at 3,000 VA.
  • Laundry circuit — one dedicated laundry branch circuit (NEC 220.52(B)) is pre-included at 1,500 VA.
Add a load below only if it is a large or dedicated circuit not covered above:
  • Electric range / wall oven (240V dedicated)
  • Electric dryer (240V dedicated)
  • Electric water heater (dedicated circuit)
  • Pool pump, spa, or hot tub
  • EV charger / Level 2 EVSE
  • Existing mini-split(s) or central A/C (nameplate MCA)
  • Any other large dedicated load not covered by standard 15/20A circuits
